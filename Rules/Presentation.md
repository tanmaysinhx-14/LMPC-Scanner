## Strategic Objective & Evaluator Psychology
This presentation is our most critical asset for the initial submission and the final "Power Judging" rounds[cite: 1]. The SIH evaluators review hundreds of ideas and suffer from severe decision fatigue[cite: 1]. We cannot rely solely on a strong technical backend; if our presentation is convoluted, lacks clarity, or fails to adhere to the formatting rules, we will lose points or face disqualification regardless of our prototype's quality[cite: 1]. 

Evaluators use strict rubrics to score us on feasibility, innovation, and sustainability, while actively penalizing unnecessary complexity[cite: 1]. This document forces us to distill our engineering concepts into rule-compliant, high-impact visual data[cite: 1].

## Strict Mandatory Constraints
*   **Slide Limit:** We are restricted to exactly six (6) slides, including the title slide[cite: 1, 2].
*   **Template Compliance:** We must use the exact section headers provided; altering the layout is grounds for disqualification[cite: 1, 2].
*   **Visual Format:** Standard text paragraphs are heavily discouraged; we must use concise bullet points, architectural diagrams, flowcharts, and infographics[cite: 1, 2].
*   **File Format:** The final export must strictly be a **PDF**; PPT or Word files will be rejected by the portal[cite: 1, 2].
*   **Skimmability:** A judge should grasp the primary takeaway of any slide within 5 seconds using high-contrast typography and bold text for key metrics[cite: 1].

## Slide-by-Slide Content Blueprint

### Slide 1: Title Page
*   **Purpose:** Purely administrative tracking.
*   **Requirements:** Problem Statement ID, Problem Title, Theme, Category, Team ID, Team Name[cite: 1, 2].

### Slide 2: Idea Title / Proposed Solution
*   **Purpose:** The "Hook."
*   **Requirements:** Detail the core concept, mechanism of action, and explicitly state our innovation[cite: 1, 2].
*   **Strategy:** Use a "Before" (Problem) and "After" (Solution) infographic. Avoid vague introductory statements entirely[cite: 1].

### Slide 3: Technical Approach
*   **Purpose:** Prove our engineering maturity and system design capability.
*   **Requirements:** Breakdown of our technology stack (Frontend, Backend, AI/ML, Cloud) and a step-by-step process flow[cite: 1, 2].
*   **Strategy:** We must use **Mermaid.js** for our architecture diagrams[cite: 1]. Manual drawing tools cause "doc-rot" if our backend changes mid-hackathon[cite: 1]. Mermaid.js allows us to write diagrams as code, generating clean SVGs that stay perfectly synchronized with our final codebase[cite: 1].

### Slide 4: Feasibility and Viability
*   **Purpose:** Demonstrate realistic project management and risk assessment.
*   **Requirements:** Feasibility analysis, anticipated bottlenecks, and mitigation strategies[cite: 1, 2].
*   **Strategy:** Evaluators hate utopian solutions[cite: 1]. Explicitly list real-world constraints (e.g., rural internet drops, API rate limits, hardware costs) and immediately provide our fallback plans (e.g., offline-first mobile architecture with background sync)[cite: 1].

### Slide 5: Impact and Benefits
*   **Purpose:** Target the "Social Relevance & Impact" rubric.
*   **Requirements:** Identify the target audience and quantifiable benefits[cite: 1, 2].
*   **Strategy:** Use grounded, numerical metrics (e.g., "Expected 20% operational cost reduction") instead of vague promises[cite: 1]. Align our solution with macro-environmental goals like the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) or Atmanirbhar Bharat to prove market readiness[cite: 1].

### Slide 6: Research and References
*   **Purpose:** Validate our approach and prove competitive advantage.
*   **Requirements:** Citations to datasets, IEEE papers, API documentation, and competitor analysis[cite: 1, 2].
